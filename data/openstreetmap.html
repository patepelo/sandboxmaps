A map of the world, made by people like you

The maps in Sandbox Maps are mostly based on OpenStreetMap (OSM). Here is what that means, and how you can help make the map better.

What is OpenStreetMap?

OpenStreetMap is a free map of the whole world. It works a bit like Wikipedia, but for maps: instead of articles, people add roads, paths, shops, parks, bus stops, opening hours and much more.

Nobody owns it, and anyone can use it. Many apps and websites are built on the same OpenStreetMap data, including CoMaps and Sandbox Maps.

Who makes it?

Millions of volunteers, from locals who know their street to people mapping from aerial photos. They all add to one shared map, so a fix made by one person helps everyone.

How the map gets onto your phone

OpenStreetMap changes every minute. From time to time, map files are made from it and published, and Sandbox Maps downloads them to your phone. That is why the app works without internet.

The map data date in About tells you when your maps were made. Anything added to OpenStreetMap after that date appears in the app once you update your maps.

Fix something yourself

Is a shop missing, or are the opening hours wrong? You can fix it right from the app:

  1. Tap the place on the map to open it.
  2. Tap Edit Place and change what is wrong.
  3. Sign in with a free OpenStreetMap account and save.

Your change shows on your phone straight away. It is also sent to OpenStreetMap, so it reaches everyone using Sandbox Maps, CoMaps and other OpenStreetMap apps after their next map update.

For bigger changes, like drawing new roads or buildings, try one of the editors below.

Recommended editors

All of these are free and edit the same OpenStreetMap, so your changes reach Sandbox Maps too. Use the same OpenStreetMap account everywhere.

StreetCompleteEasyAndroid

Answers simple questions about places around you while you walk, like "Is this bench made of wood?". A fun way to start, with no map skills needed.

streetcomplete.app
Every DoorEasyAndroid

Made for adding and updating shops, cafés, entrances and other points of interest on the spot.

every-door.app
iDMediumWeb browser

The editor built into the OpenStreetMap website. Good for drawing roads, paths and buildings on a bigger screen, with a built-in walkthrough for beginners.

openstreetmap.org/edit
VespucciAdvancedAndroid

A full editor on your phone or tablet, for detailed mapping out in the field.

vespucci.io
JOSMAdvancedComputer

The most powerful editor, used by experienced mappers for large or complex changes.

josm.openstreetmap.de

Free and open

OpenStreetMap data is open: anyone may use it, as long as they credit OpenStreetMap and share improvements under the same terms (the Open Database License). That is why you see "© OpenStreetMap contributors" in apps like this one.
